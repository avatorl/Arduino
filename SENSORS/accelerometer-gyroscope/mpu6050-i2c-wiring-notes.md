# MPU6050 and I2C Bus Notes

You are right, I should have answered from the schematics only.

The three modules all use I²C, so their `SDA` wires join together and their `SCL` wires join together. Every module must also share `GND` with the Nano.

The key rule is: there must be only one compatible pull-up voltage on each common I²C bus segment.

## Module 1: VL53L0X

Its sensor IC operates at `2.8 V` (`VDD`), generated by the onboard regulator from `VIN`.

- `R3` and `R4` pull the sensor-side I²C signals to `2.8 V`.
- `R1` and `R2` pull the connector-side I²C signals to `VIN`.
- `Q1A` and `Q1B` are BSS138 MOSFET level shifters between the two sides.

Therefore, when you power `VIN` from Nano `5V`, you may connect its header `SDA` and `SCL` directly to Nano `SDA`/`SCL`. The board translates between Nano-side 5 V logic and sensor-side 2.8 V logic.

## Module 2: TCS34725 color sensor

This works on the same principle.

- The onboard RT9193 regulator converts `VIN` to `3.3 V`.
- The TCS34725 itself uses `3.3 V`.
- The BSS138 MOSFETs translate I²C between the sensor’s 3.3 V side and the connector’s `VIN` side.
- Its `SCL_5V` and `SDA_5V` header pins are intended for a 5 V controller when `VIN = 5 V`.

So this one also connects directly to the Nano:

```text
Nano 5V  -> TCS34725 VIN
Nano GND -> TCS34725 GND
Nano A4  -> TCS34725 SDA_5V
Nano A5  -> TCS34725 SCL_5V
```

## Module 3: MPU6050

This board has a regulator, but it has no MOSFET I²C level shifter.

- `5V` power can enter its regulator.
- The MPU6050 and its I²C pull-ups operate at `3.3 V`.
- Its `2.2 kΩ` pull-ups connect SDA and SCL directly to the 3.3 V rail.

Therefore its SDA/SCL pins are a 3.3 V bus, even when you supply the module from Nano `5V`.

Do not connect this module’s SDA/SCL directly onto the same bus as the first two modules when they are powered from 5 V. The VL53L0X and TCS34725 boards pull their Nano-facing I²C sides toward 5 V. That would feed 5 V into the MPU6050’s unshifted I²C pins.

## Correct combined wiring

```text
Nano 5V  -> VL53L0X VIN
          -> TCS34725 VIN
          -> MPU6050 VCC / VIN       (only if that breakout accepts 5 V input)

Nano GND -> all three GND pins

Nano A4  -> VL53L0X SDA
          -> TCS34725 SDA_5V

Nano A5  -> VL53L0X SCL
          -> TCS34725 SCL_5V

Nano A4/A5 -> 5 V side of an external I²C level shifter
3.3 V side -> MPU6050 SDA/SCL
```

Power the level shifter as:

```text
Level shifter HV -> Nano 5V
Level shifter LV -> Nano 3.3V
Level shifter GND -> Nano GND
```

The external level shifter keeps the Nano/VL53L0X/TCS34725 bus at 5 V while keeping the MPU6050 bus at 3.3 V.

One separate issue remains: the VL53L0X and TCS34725 both default to I²C address `0x29`. Their voltages can be made safe, but they still cannot both communicate simultaneously until one device’s address changes. The VL53L0X provides `XSHUT`, which lets an Arduino start it separately and assign it a different address.

## Update: MPU6050 alone on Nano 5V

That is expected. The MPU6050 board can be powered from Nano `5V` because its onboard `3.3 V` LDO regulates the supply before it reaches the MPU6050.

It can also often work with Nano SDA/SCL connected directly because I²C pins are open-drain:

- The Nano’s I²C hardware pulls SDA/SCL low or releases them.
- It does not normally drive a 5 V high level.
- The MPU6050 module’s `2.2 kΩ` resistors then pull the released lines up to `3.3 V`.
- A typical ATmega328P will usually recognize 3.3 V as HIGH, although this is marginal against the official 5 V logic-high specification.

So, by itself, this arrangement is common and normally works:

```text
Nano 5V  -> MPU6050 VCC
Nano GND -> MPU6050 GND
Nano A4  -> MPU6050 SDA
Nano A5  -> MPU6050 SCL
```

The concern arises only when you add the other two modules. Their level-shifter boards pull the Arduino-facing SDA/SCL bus up to 5 V through their 10 kΩ resistors. That makes the MPU6050 SDA/SCL pins see 5 V, instead of their own 3.3 V pull-up level.

So:

- MPU6050 alone: likely fine in practice.
- MPU6050 + VL53L0X/TCS34725, all direct: not recommended because the 5 V pull-ups become connected to the MPU6050 I²C pins.
- All modules through an external I²C level shifter: electrically proper.

A practical compromise, often used with these hobby breakouts, is to remove or disable the 5 V-side I²C pull-ups on the VL53L0X and TCS34725 boards, then let the MPU6050’s 3.3 V pull-ups serve the whole bus. But that only works if the level-shifter boards correctly pass a 3.3 V bus level to the Nano side and the Nano reliably accepts that level. An external level shifter is the cleaner solution.
